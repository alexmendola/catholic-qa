## Explanation

The confusion usually comes from a real observation: Catholics do pray to Mary, build shrines to her, and speak of her in elevated terms that can sound like worship to someone from outside the tradition. So the question is fair. But the answer rests on a critical distinction the Church has always maintained.

Worship – latria in the classical theological vocabulary – is due to God alone. It is the total self-offering, the acknowledgment of absolute sovereignty, that belongs exclusively to the Creator. Catholics offer this to the Trinity and to no one else.

Honour – dulia – is a different thing entirely. It is the reverence given to a holy person because of their relationship with God. Catholics honour the saints the way any believer honours a person of extraordinary holiness. Mary receives a special form of this honour – hyperdulia – because of her unique role as the Mother of God, but this remains categorically different from worship.

This distinction is not a modern invention. It was articulated clearly at the Second Council of Nicaea in 787 AD and has been the consistent teaching of the Church ever since. Scripture itself models it: when the angel Gabriel greets Mary as "full of grace" (Luke 1:28, RSVCE) and when Elizabeth calls her "the mother of my Lord" (Luke 1:43), both are offering profound honour to a human being – not worship.

The Catechism is precise on this point: Catholic devotion to Mary "differs essentially from the adoration which is given to the incarnate Word and equally to the Father and the Holy Spirit" (CCC 971).

## What to say

'Catholics don't worship Mary – we honour her, the same way you'd honour your own mother, just more so. Worship belongs to God alone; that's never changed. The Church has actually been very precise about this distinction for over a thousand years. Honouring the woman God chose to carry His Son isn't the same as worshipping her – any more than honouring a hero is the same as worshipping them.'

## If they push back

They may say: "But you pray to her – that's worship."

"It's worth separating two things that often get confused: prayer and worship. Prayer simply means communication – asking someone to intercede. When Paul writes to the Romans, he asks them to strive together with him in their prayers to God on his behalf (Romans 15:30, RSVCE). He's asking human beings to intercede for him. That's not worship – it's asking for prayer. Catholics believe Mary is alive in heaven, and asking her to pray for us works on exactly the same principle as asking a friend here on earth. The form is different; the logic is identical."
